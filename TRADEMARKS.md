## BinaryPaper

"BinaryPaper" and the BinaryPaper logo are trademarks of the BinaryPaper project.

### You may

- state truthfully that your implementation **is compatible with** a named BinaryPaper
  capsule protocol version — for example, "compatible with BinaryPaper Capsule
  Protocol 1.0";
- state truthfully that your implementation **passes** a named version of this
  conformance suite — for example, "passes BinaryPaper Recovery Kit conformance suite
  1.0.0";
- use the name descriptively to say what your software reads or writes; and
- fork this repository and say what it is a fork of.

### You may not

- imply that BinaryPaper endorses, sponsors, certifies, or is affiliated with your
  project;
- use "BinaryPaper" in your product name, package name, domain name, organization
  name, or logo in a way that suggests it is an official release;
- describe your build of this tool as "the official BinaryPaper recovery tool"; or
- use the BinaryPaper logo or visual branding at all.

The distinction is between *describing compatibility*, which is a factual claim you
are welcome to make, and *borrowing identity*, which is not.

### If you fork

Please rename the executable and the package. A fork that keeps the original name and
ships different behavior makes it impossible for a user holding a printed backup to
know what they are running — which is the exact failure mode this project exists to
prevent.

## Compatibility claims are factual, not certification

There is no certification program, no exclusive mark, and no application process.
"Passes suite 1.0.0" is a claim you make and that anyone can verify by running the
suite. BinaryPaper may publish its own machine-readable conformance reports for its
own applications; those are evidence about those applications, not a grant to anyone
else.
